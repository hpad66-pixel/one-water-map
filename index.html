How It Is All Contained

Two zones: your Mac on the left, GitHub on the right, joined only by the seal. Two books on one lane, the course house, and the software estate, all running the same pattern. The hats from CLAUDE.md shown where they actually work. Updated August 1, 2026.

Start here · before any session, in any tool, connect these folders
  1. ~/dev/hardeep-soul · always. The shipyard: both books' manuscripts, both dictation wells, the white paper, every output. If only one folder is connected, it is this one.
  2. ~/dev/the-steersman-book · only when publishing or refreshing the Book One reading edition.
  3. ~/dev/one-water-knowledge-graph · only when updating the white paper's public copy.
  4. ~/dev/one-water-map · only when updating this map.
  5. ~/dev/owos-learning-content · the course house: only when building or shipping courses (AI Masterclass, Knowledge Graph course, the trainers).
  6. ~/dev/onewater-os-platform · the software estate: only when building applications (PumpOS, RegOS, FogOS, the I & I module). It carries its own CLAUDE.md.
The ritual: connect · dictate into the well, verbatim and dated · author in the shipyard only · seal.
The seal is one command: bash ~/dev/hardeep-soul/scripts/seal_all.sh "what changed". It commits and pushes all five content repos at once; the software estate seals inside its own protocol. Until it runs, changes exist only on your Mac. In Cowork, connect the folders at session start; in Claude Code or Codex, open ~/dev/hardeep-soul, whose CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md carry this exact protocol. Dictations land verbatim in outputs/book-evidence/, book-one/ or book-two/, before any shaping. You never have to remember the command: the words "push everything to git" are enough. In Claude Code or Codex the agent runs the seal itself; in Cowork the agent stages everything and hands you this exact line ready to paste. scripts/SEAL-README.md adds two conveniences: a one-word alias, seal, and an optional nightly auto-seal at 9 pm.

How to read this, in plain words

The dashed vertical line is the most important line on the page. Left of it is your Mac: six ordinary folders under ~/dev where all the work actually happens. Right of it is GitHub: the sealed twin of each folder, holding every version of every file forever. The only bridge between the two sides is the seal, one command, bash ~/dev/hardeep-soul/scripts/seal_all.sh "what changed", which commits and pushes all five content repos at once. Until the seal runs, new work exists only on your Mac. After it runs, the two sides match, and history has one more permanent entry.

The hats at the top of the shipyard answer the agents question plainly. Granth, Bodhi, the gates, and the crew are roles defined in CLAUDE.md, not robots running on their own. Nothing acts until a session, in Cowork, Claude Code, or Codex, opens the folder, or until you run one of their scripts in scripts/. When something does act, it wears exactly one lane's hat: Granth may only write book/, Bodhi only book-bubble/, the gates only report. The folder itself teaches the rules to any tool that opens it.

The two books now run identical lanes, and that is the point of drawing Book Two even though it is mostly empty. Well two is open and waiting; three chapters already sit in book-bubble; the figures and compiled stations are dashed outlines because they do not exist yet. The day you start dictating The Bubble Syndrome, the material lands in well two, Bodhi's lane fills exactly the way Granth's did, and when it compiles, the dashed future repo on both sides becomes real. Nothing about the process changes; only the dashes become solid.

And your newest question has the same answer as every other one: the architecture is a pattern, not a building, so anything you make next simply gets the same stations. The AI Masterclass already sits in the course house, owos-learning-content, which is its own shipyard: courses are authored there, sealed there, and rendered to LearnWorlds as SCORM the way books render to GitHub Pages. The Knowledge Graph course does not exist yet, but it is the least blank blank-slate imaginable: its full curriculum blueprint, six modules and twenty-six lessons with a build prompt for every lesson, already exists as Appendix G of the white paper, and a seed folder is already waiting in the course house. I & I shows how one product name divides across two estates: its course content lives in the course house today, and the I & I application module will live in the software estate, onewater-os-platform, beside PumpOS, RegOS, and FogOS, where the gates are tests and the render is the running app. However many courses or modules you add, nothing new has to be invented; a new thing gets a well or a spec, a lane, a compile, a seal, and a render.

Two things on your Mac never reach git, on purpose: the desktop gallery, which holds convenience mirrors of the artifacts for one-click viewing in the Claude app, and my cloud workbench, which is wiped after every session. The rule that protects you is printed inside their box: if it matters, it gets a path in a repo. Everything you care about already has one.

And the answer to the standing question: no, you do not have to remember to push, and you do not have to ask me. Every session that changes files will end with me saying the seal is due, naming the repos touched, with the command ready to paste. Run it then, or whenever you like; nothing is lost in between, it is simply local until sealed. If you ever want it done on the spot, the words "push everything to git" are enough: in Claude Code or Codex the agent runs the seal itself, and in Cowork I stage everything and hand you the line ready to paste. And if one day you want the seal to run without your hands at all, scripts/ holds a nightly auto-seal you can switch on; that means letting an automation use your git credentials, a decision to make deliberately, not by default.
